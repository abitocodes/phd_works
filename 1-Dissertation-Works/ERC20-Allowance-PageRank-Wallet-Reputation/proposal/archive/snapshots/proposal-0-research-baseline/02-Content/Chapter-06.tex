\section*{6 \textbf{Limitations}}
\label{sec:limitations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{6 Limitations}

While this study is designed to rigorously evaluate EndorseRank against Adaptive Weighted PageRank, we acknowledge several inherent limitations and potential threats to validity:


\proposalSubheading{1. Data Coverage and Quality}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Event Completeness:} We rely on public event logs (Approve, Permit, Transfer, GMX \textbf{PositionDecrease}) from Arbitrum One archives or BigQuery. Some missing or misindexed events (e.g., due to chain reorganizations or node sync issues) may bias graph construction or GMX outcome aggregation.
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Proxy Limitations:} Trading-success proxies (close-success count, realized gain proxy) are domain-intuition measures, not external ground-truth credit labels. High correlation does not by itself establish predictive validity for unrelated under-collateralized lending outcomes studied in prior lending-focused scoring work (e.g., Ghosh et al., 2024).
\end{itemize}
\proposalSubheading{2. Temporal Scope}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Fixed Window}: Limiting analysis to a single six-month window may not capture longer-term reputation dynamics or market regime shifts. Results may differ for other periods, especially during high volatility or GMX protocol upgrades.
\end{itemize}
\proposalSubheading{3. Graph Modeling Assumptions}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Approval Semantics}: We assume that any nonzero approve/permit rep- resents a substantive trust endorsement. In practice, users may issue
\end{itemize}
``spend-all'' approvals by default or interact via custodial services, which dilute the trust signal.


\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Edge Weight Aggregation}: Summing all transfers or using only the latest allowance ignores the provenance of funds and may obscure multi- token or multi-chain interactions.
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{GMX Trading Semantics}: Profitable \textbf{PositionDecrease} closes reflect realized PnL on perpetual swap positions, not loan repayment. The credit analogy is interpretive; wash trading, copy trading, and automated strategies may inflate proxies without reflecting genuine trustworthiness.
\end{itemize}
\proposalSubheading{4. Algorithmic Simplifications}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Fixed Damping Factor}: We apply a single damping factor (d = 0.85) uniformly; in reality, the optimal factor may vary by token, wallet type, or network segment.
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Unmodeled Attributes}: EndorseRank ignores off-chain factors (e.g., KYC status, IP geolocation) and non-ERC-20 relationships (e.g., NFT approvals, cross-chain bridges), which may also influence trust.
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Minimum Close Filter}: Requiring $\text{total\_closes} \geq 3$ excludes novice or inactive traders and may introduce selection bias in the matched sample.
\end{itemize}
\proposalSubheading{5. Generalizability}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Protocol Specificity}: We focus on GMX V2 perpetual swap outcomes on Arbitrum One. EndorseRank's alignment may differ for other perpetual exchanges (e.g., dYdX, Hyperliquid) or non-trading use cases.
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Asset Bias}: High-volume tokens (e.g., USDC, WETH) dominate graph structure; smaller or emerging tokens may yield sparse endorsement net- works less amenable to PageRank.
\end{itemize}
\proposalSubheading{6. Adversarial Manipulation and Sybil Attacks}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Gaming the Graph}: Malicious actors could issue dummy approvals or orchestrate circular allowance patterns to inflate their EndorseRank score. Similarly, coordinated small profitable closes could inflate close-success count. Detecting and mitigating such Sybil strategies require additional safe- guards beyond the scope of this study.
\end{itemize}
\proposalSubheading{7. Computational Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbullet] \textbf{Scalability Limitations}: Although EndorseRank is more efficient than AWP on our sample, real-time computation on the full Arbitrum transfer graph
\end{itemize}
(millions of wallets) may still demand distributed computing or approximation techniques.
