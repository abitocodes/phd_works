02 · Problem & gap

Transfer graphs miss endorsement semantics

Current practice

  • PageRank-style scores (e.g., AWP) weight token transfers as economic trust.
  • Transfers are noisy: trading, routing, airdrops, bots—not always endorsement.
  • Large transfer graphs raise data-processing, memory, and convergence cost for repeated scoring.

Research gap

  • ERC-20 approve / permit encode explicit spending authority until revoked.
  • Prior graph reputation systems largely ignore allowance edges as first-class links.
  • Attestation tools (e.g., Passport) address personhood, not graded financial ranking.

Central problem: Can an approve/permit graph on Arbitrum One yield equal or better alignment with GMX V2 trading success than transfer-based PageRank, at lower computational cost?

06 · Method overview

Same wallets, two graphs, shared validation

Arbitrum logs (BQ)→ GMX PositionDecrease→ Qualified wallets (≥3 closes)→ Approval graph / Transfer graph→ EndorseRank vs AWP→ ρ / τ & runtime

EndorseRank

  • Nodes: wallets; edges: owner → spender
  • Weight: latest nonzero allowance (decimal-adjusted)
  • Damping d = 0.85; no time decay

AWP / transfer baseline

  • Edges: ERC-20 transfers; weights by transfer value
  • AWP: logistic time-decay on transfer age
  • Compared on the identical GMX-active cohort and window

Validation uses GMX V2 trading-success proxies (close-success count, realized gain, success rate)—domain-intuition measures, not loan-repayment ground truth.

08 · Validation choice

Why we must use GMX PositionDecrease data

Ideal labels (scarce)

Under-collateralized lending defaults / liquidations would be the most direct credit-risk labels—but on Arbitrum One, observable unsecured or under-collateralized lending volume is insufficient for a stable evaluation set.

Multi-chain alternative

Pulling default labels from other chains would break the single-chain design and inflate data-engineering cost and wallet-identity ambiguity.

Adopted solution

GMX V2 perpetual long/short PositionDecrease outcomes provide dense, wallet-level realized PnL on the same chain as the reputation graphs.

Profitable close (basePnlUsd > 0, excluding liquidation) is treated as a functional analog of successfully managing collateralized leveraged exposure—not as proof of creditworthiness.

Proposal stance: domain-intuition proxies for construct alignment (Cronbach & Meehl), following Do et al.’s proxy-validation pattern—not loan-repayment ground truth.

Limitation (proposal Ch.6)

A profitable perpetual trader is not the same construct as a good unsecured borrower. Alignment may also differ on other venues (e.g., dYdX, Hyperliquid).

11 · Interpretation guide

What these parameters allow us to know

Sampling (window, m, cohort)

  • Window + T_obs: fix which events exist and how old transfers are for AWP.
  • m = 3: success-rate is not dominated by a single lucky/unlucky close.
  • Same cohort for both methods: τ differences reflect graph/method, not unequal samples.

Solver / graph (d, tol, k, t₀, weights)

  • d = 0.85: how much score comes from neighbors vs uniform teleport.
  • Tolerance / I_max: runtime reflects structure, not early stopping.
  • k, t₀: how strongly AWP prefers recent transfers; EndorseRank keeps the latest live allowance.

Design principle

Pre-register knobs, then measure alignment and efficiency. Robustness sweeps (damping, top tokens, sample size, weight transforms) test sensitivity; they do not redefine the primary setting after seeing τ.

Inclusion rules

  • Profitable close: basePnlUsd > 0
  • Exclude liquidations: orderType ≠ 7
  • Source: GMX V2 EventEmitter on Arbitrum One

What we are not claiming

  • Not loan-repayment ground truth
  • Not identity resolution across wallets
  • Wash/copy/auto trading can inflate proxies (limitation)
