\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Clarity contracts are published as source, so a registry for them can be built from chain data alone, but it needs a ranking that resists fake popularity. EndorseRank cannot be moved to Stacks unchanged because Stacks has no allowance. The allowance relation splits into two parts: post-conditions record which contracts a wallet let move its assets, and delegation and dependency references in code record which contracts a contract relies on. AWP can be applied to Stacks transfers as it stands.

Moving wallets out of the graph and into the restart distribution is what makes the ranking resist Sybil attacks. With a uniform restart, a wallet--contract graph turns PageRank into a count of wallets, and dependency PageRank turns it into a count of deployed contracts; both are cheap to inflate. With a restart weighted by fees, an attacker pays for influence in proportion to what honest wallets have paid, and contracts that nobody authorizes and no honest contract references receive no score at all. The registry built on this ranking separates referencing a contract from copying it, ties scopes and versions to deployer addresses, groups clones by hash and verifies code hashes on chain.

The evaluation set out in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} will show whether the higher cost of manipulation holds on Stacks mainnet and whether it comes without a loss of agreement with later adoption. Both outcomes are reported whatever their direction.
