\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Clarity contracts are published as source, so a registry for them can be built from chain data alone, but it needs a ranking that resists fake popularity. EndorseRank cannot be moved to Stacks unchanged because Stacks has no allowance. The allowance relation splits into two parts: post-conditions record which contracts a wallet let move its assets, and delegation and dependency references in code record which contracts a contract relies on. AWP can be applied to Stacks transfers as it stands.

The propositions point to one change: wallets leave the graph and act only through the restart distribution. With a uniform restart, the part of a contract's score that comes from wallets is a count of wallets, and dependency PageRank grows with the number of contracts deployed around a target; both are cheap to inflate. With a restart weighted by fees, splitting a budget across wallets buys nothing, and a group of contracts that no wallet authorizes and no outside contract references receives no score. The synthetic example shows these effects on a made-up graph. The registry built on this ranking separates referencing a contract from copying it, ties scopes and versions to deployer addresses, groups clones by hash and checks code hashes on chain.

Whether the higher cost of manipulation holds on Stacks mainnet, and whether it comes without a loss of agreement with later adoption, is what the evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} tests. Both outcomes will be reported whatever their direction.
