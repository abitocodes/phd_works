\section{\PCER}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Notation}

Let $T_0$ be the freeze time of the ranking, $C$ the set of contracts deployed on Stacks mainnet up to $T_0$, and $\mathcal{W}=(T_0-\Delta,T_0]$ an observation window, with $\Delta=180$ days as in the ERC-20 study \cite{kwon2026endorserank}. For a transaction $\tau$ in $\mathcal{W}$, $o(\tau)$ is its origin (a standard principal, called a wallet below), $c(\tau)\in C$ the contract named in its payload when the payload is a contract call, $f(\tau)$ its fee, and $\mathrm{out}_a(\tau)$ the amount of asset $a$ that $o(\tau)$ sent or burned during $\tau$, read from the transaction's asset events. $\mathrm{Auth}(\tau)$ is the set of assets for which $\tau$ carries an authorizing post-condition under the rules of Section~\ref{sec:mapping-rules}, including blanket authorizations from Allow mode. $p(a)$ is the price of asset $a$ at $T_0$ in a common unit; it is zero for assets without a reliable price and, in the primary analysis, for non-fungible tokens.

\subsection{Authorization records and the latest-state rule}
\label{sec:method-records}

\begin{definition}[Authorization record]
For a wallet $u$, a contract $c$ and an asset $a$, let $\tau^{*}(u,c,a)$ be the latest successful transaction in $\mathcal{W}$ with $o(\tau)=u$, $c(\tau)=c$ and $a\in\mathrm{Auth}(\tau)$. Its value is $v(u,c,a)=p(a)\,\mathrm{out}_a(\tau^{*})$. The authorization of $c$ by $u$ is $A(u,c)=\sum_a v(u,c,a)$, the set of contracts $u$ has authorized is $C_u=\{c : \tau^{*}(u,c,a)\text{ exists for some }a\}$, and the share of $c$ in $u$'s endorsement is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:share}
s_u(c)=
\begin{cases}
A(u,c)\big/\sum_{c'\in C_u}A(u,c') & \text{if the denominator is positive,}\\
1/|C_u| & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Two choices differ from a literal copy of EndorseRank. First, the value is the amount that actually left the wallet, not the bound written in the post-condition. In a successful transaction an upper bound is never below the outflow, and a bound, like an ERC-20 allowance, can be set far above what the wallet holds at no cost; the realized outflow requires the asset to move. Second, the latest-state rule applies per wallet, contract and asset, as EndorseRank applies it per owner, spender and token. A wallet that calls the same contract a thousand times counts once, with the value of its most recent call, so repeated calls from one wallet do not add weight.

\subsection{Why the restart has to change}

Moving EndorseRank to Stacks without further change would mean a graph with wallets and contracts as nodes, edges $u\to c$ weighted by $A(u,c)$, edges between contracts, and a uniform restart. The following proposition shows what such a graph measures.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform restart counts wallets]
\label{prop:bipartite}
Let $G$ have node set $W\cup C$ with $N$ nodes, edges $u\to c$ from wallets to contracts with transition probabilities $s_u(c)$, edges among contracts, and the uniform restart $\pi_v=1/N$. Then every wallet has the same score $r_W=(1-d+d\,\delta)/N$, where $\delta$ is the score held by dangling nodes, and the score that wallets pass to a contract $c$ is $d\,r_W\sum_{u}s_u(c)$. Adding $k$ wallets that each authorize only a contract $x$ adds $k\,d\,r_W$ to the score passed to $x$, whatever amounts they authorize.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
No edge ends at a wallet: contracts do not originate transactions, and edges between contracts end at contracts. By Equation~\eqref{eq:pagerank} each wallet's score is therefore $(1-d)/N+d\,\delta/N$, which does not depend on the wallet. A wallet $u$ passes $d\,r_W$ along its out-edges in proportions $s_u(c)$, so the total passed to $c$ is $d\,r_W\sum_u s_u(c)$. A wallet that authorizes only $x$ has $s_u(x)=1$.
\end{proof}

Under a uniform restart, the authorized amounts only decide how each wallet splits a fixed contribution among its contracts. The part of a contract's score that comes from wallets is a count of wallets in which each wallet's single vote is divided among the contracts it uses, and a Sybil wallet costs one transaction fee and brings a full vote. Value weighting does not change this. The proposition also suggests one reason why, in the ERC-20 study, raw in-degree counts matched held-out approvals better than EndorseRank \cite{kwon2026endorserank}: an allowance graph in which few spenders are also owners is close to bipartite, and on such a graph the walk adds little to a count.

\subsection{Wallet weights and the restart distribution}

We therefore remove wallets from the node set and let them act only through the restart distribution, with a weight $\omega(u)$ that the attacker has to pay for:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:restart}
\omega(u)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{W}:\,o(\tau)=u}\min\bigl(f(\tau),f_{\max}\bigr),
\qquad
\pi_c=\frac{\sum_{u}\omega(u)\,s_u(c)}{\sum_{u:\,C_u\neq\emptyset}\omega(u)} .
\end{equation}
The weight of a wallet is the fees of all transactions it originated in the window, each capped at $f_{\max}$, the 99th percentile of fees in $\mathcal{W}$. Fees are counted for the origin even when a sponsor paid them, since sponsored fees are still a cost to the party that wants the endorsement. Equation~\eqref{eq:restart} is the restart a walk would see if it started at a wallet chosen in proportion to $\omega$ and took one step along the authorization edges.

\begin{proposition}[Splitting a fee budget gains nothing]
\label{prop:split}
Suppose an attacker spends a fee budget $F$ through wallets that authorize only $x$. Under Equation~\eqref{eq:restart} their contribution to the numerator of $\pi_x$ is at most $F$, however the budget is divided among wallets. If instead $\omega(u)=g(\text{fees of }u)$ for a concave $g$ with $g(0)=0$, dividing $F$ equally among $k$ wallets contributes $k\,g(F/k)\geq g(F)$; with $\omega\equiv 1$ it contributes $k$ for a cost of $k$ minimum fees.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first claim follows from linearity and the cap, which can only lower a counted fee. A concave $g$ with $g(0)=0$ is subadditive, so $g(F)=g\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}F/k\bigr)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}g(F/k)$.
\end{proof}

Linear weights make influence proportional to money spent on transactions, which an attacker cannot avoid by creating identities. The price is that a wallet that transacts a lot counts more than one that transacts rarely; a one-wallet-one-vote rule would need an identity layer that Stacks does not have, and Cheng and Friedman's result says that no symmetric rule escapes this \cite{cheng2005sybilproof}. Against $\omega\equiv1$ an attacker buys one unit of influence with one minimum fee. Against Equation~\eqref{eq:restart}, matching the influence of $n$ honest wallets costs what those wallets paid, so the cost rises by the ratio of the mean fee of an honest wallet to the minimum fee. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} measures this ratio on mainnet. The sensitivity analysis also uses capital-days, the time-averaged STX balance of a wallet over $\mathcal{W}$: it is split-invariant like fees, and the same coins cannot sit in two wallets at once.

Stacks miners collect transaction fees, so a miner could pay fees to itself. The cap $f_{\max}$ limits what a single self-paid transaction can buy; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to this.

\subsection{Edges between contracts}

Let $E_{\mathrm{del}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{dep}}$ be the delegation and dependency edges of Section~\ref{sec:mapping}. They are extracted from source and from governance transactions as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dependency:} $A\to B$ if $A$ contains \code{contract-call?} with target $B$ written as an absolute principal, as a relative principal \code{.name} (a contract of the same deployer), or as a constant bound to such a principal (Clarity~5); or if $A$ has \code{impl-trait} or \code{use-trait} referring to a trait defined in $B$.
\item \textbf{Delegation:} $A\to B$ if a resolved call from $A$ to $B$ lies in the body of \code{as-contract?}, legacy \code{as-contract} or \code{restrict-assets?}; or if $A$ follows the ExecutorDAO pattern and the latest \code{set-extension} call in $A$'s history enables $B$.
\item \textbf{Excluded:} calls whose target is a trait-typed argument, self-loops, and references to contracts that do not exist at $T_0$.
\end{itemize}

An edge in both sets counts as delegation. Weights are $\beta_{\mathrm{del}}=2$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{dep}}=1$, fixed in advance with $\{1,2,4\}$ for $\beta_{\mathrm{del}}$ in the sensitivity analysis. Delegation weighs more because it puts assets or authority at the callee's disposal, which is the distinction between authorization and use on which EndorseRank rests. The transition matrix is $P_{AB}=\beta(A,B)/\sum_{B'}\beta(A,B')$.

\subsection{Score}

\PCER\ is the solution of Equation~\eqref{eq:pagerank} on the node set $C$ with transition matrix $P$ and restart $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:restart}, with $d=0.85$, an $\ell_1$ tolerance of $10^{-8}$ and at most 300 iterations, the settings of the ERC-20 study \cite{kwon2026endorserank}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pcer} lists the steps. Contracts with zero score have no evidence of use; the registry lists them but does not rank them.

\begin{proposition}[Score of an attacker's contracts]
\label{prop:region}
For a set $S\subseteq C$ let $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(S)=\sum_{v\notin S}r_v P_{vS}$ be the score that flows into $S$ along edges from contracts outside $S$, where $P_{vS}=\sum_{y\in S}P_{vy}$. Then
\begin{equation}
r(S)\;\leq\;\frac{\pi(S)}{1-d}+\frac{d}{1-d}\,\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(S).
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\pi(S)=0$ and no contract outside $S$ has an edge into $S$, then $r(S)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:pagerank} over $S$ gives $r(S)=(1-d)\pi(S)+d\sum_{v}r_vP_{vS}+d\,\delta\,\pi(S)$. Nodes of $S$ with out-edges send at most all of their score into $S$ and dangling nodes send none, so $\sum_{v\in S}r_vP_{vS}\leq r(S)-\delta_S$, where $\delta_S$ is the dangling score in $S$. Hence $(1-d)\,r(S)\leq(1-d)\pi(S)+d\,\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(S)+d\,(\delta\,\pi(S)-\delta_S)$. Since $\delta\leq1$ and $\delta_S\geq0$, the last term is at most $d\,\pi(S)$, which gives the bound. If $\pi(S)=0$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(S)=0$, the inequality reads $r(S)\leq 0$.
\end{proof}

The two terms on the right are the two things an attacker must obtain. $\pi(S)$ is bought with fees on transactions that authorize contracts in $S$, at the price set by Proposition~\ref{prop:split}. $\Phi_{\mathrm{in}}(S)$ requires contracts that already have score to reference $S$ in their code or to enable it as an extension, which are decisions of other developers and of governance; these are the attack edges of the social-graph defenses \cite{yu2006sybilguard,cao2012sybilrank}. Deploying contracts that reference a target adds members to $S$ but raises neither term. Under a uniform restart, by contrast, every deployed contract brings restart mass $1/|C|$ with it, so the bound grows with the number of contracts the attacker deploys.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{\PCER}
\label{alg:pcer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require contracts $C$ with source at $T_0$; transactions in $\mathcal{W}$; prices $p$; parameters $d,\beta_{\mathrm{del}},\beta_{\mathrm{dep}},f_{\max}$
\Ensure scores $\mathbf{r}$ on $C$
\For{each successful contract call $\tau\in\mathcal{W}$ and each $a\in\mathrm{Auth}(\tau)$}
  \State keep $\tau$ as $\tau^{*}(o(\tau),c(\tau),a)$ if it is later than the stored one
\EndFor
\State $A(u,c)\gets\sum_a p(a)\,\mathrm{out}_a(\tau^{*}(u,c,a))$; compute $s_u(c)$ by Equation~\eqref{eq:share}
\State $\omega(u)\gets\sum_{\tau:\,o(\tau)=u}\min(f(\tau),f_{\max})$; compute $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ by Equation~\eqref{eq:restart}
\State parse each contract; resolve literal, relative and constant targets; collect $E_{\mathrm{dep}}$ and $E_{\mathrm{del}}$; add enabled extensions to $E_{\mathrm{del}}$
\State $P_{AB}\gets\beta(A,B)/\sum_{B'}\beta(A,B')$
\State $\mathbf{r}\gets\boldsymbol{\pi}$
\Repeat
  \State $\mathbf{r}'\gets(1-d)\boldsymbol{\pi}+dP^{\top}\mathbf{r}+d\,\delta(\mathbf{r})\,\boldsymbol{\pi}$; swap $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{r}'$
\Until{$\lVert\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'\rVert_1<10^{-8}$ or 300 iterations}
\State \Return $\mathbf{r}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{A synthetic illustration}
\label{sec:method-toy}

To show the propositions at work we built a small synthetic graph (script in the paper's repository; parameters in Appendix~\ref{app:toy}). It has \ToyContracts\ honest contracts, among them a SIP-010 trait, six tokens, three pools that pay out tokens inside \code{as-contract?}, a router, a vault and a DAO, and \ToyWallets\ honest wallets that authorize one to three entry contracts and pay on average \ToyMeanFee\ times the minimum fee over the window. An attacker deploys a token $x$ and tries to lift it into the top \ToyTop\ by three attacks: A1 deploys contracts that each call $x$ (cost \ToyDeployFee\ fee units per contract); A2 creates wallets that each authorize $x$ once at the minimum fee; A3 calls the honest router with $x$ as its trait argument. Each baseline fails against one attack. Counting callers falls to the wallet farm (A2) at \ToyCostCallersATwo\ units. Dependency PageRank with a uniform restart, the teaRank pattern, falls to a single farm contract (A1, \ToyCostDepPRAOne\ units) because each deployed contract brings its own restart mass. EndorseRank with a uniform restart falls to both: \ToyCostERUniformATwo\ wallets suffice, close to the count of callers as Proposition~\ref{prop:bipartite} predicts, and farm contracts work too because they are nodes with restart mass. \PCER\ is not moved by A1 at any size, as Proposition~\ref{prop:region} requires, and A2 costs \ToyCostPCERATwo\ units, about \ToyRatioATwo\ times the cost under the uniform restart and close to the mean fee of an honest wallet (\ToyMeanFee\ units). A3 moves the token only when run-time trait bindings are added as router edges. In this graph that attack costs more than A2, because honest calls already bind the router's tokens hundreds of times; a router whose score comes mostly from delegation rather than from calls would be cheaper to capture.

\subsection{Families, packages and search}
\label{sec:method-search}

\textbf{Clone families.} Two contracts belong to the same family when their normalized sources have the same hash. Normalization removes comments and whitespace and replaces string and numeric literals, literal principals and the names of defined tokens, maps and variables with placeholders, then hashes the token stream. Contracts with identical code bodies also share the value of \code{contract-hash?}. Near-duplicates found by MinHash \cite{broder1997resemblance} with a Jaccard similarity of at least 0.9 are shown as related but not merged. A family is represented by its member with the highest score, earliest deployment breaking ties, and the family is ranked by that member's score. Using the maximum rather than the sum means that a thousand copies of a contract add nothing to its family.

\textbf{Packages.} A package is a lineage of contracts declared by its publisher (Section~\ref{sec:registry}). It is ranked by the score of its latest version, with older versions listed under it.

\textbf{Search.} Following Equation~\eqref{eq:pkgsite}, a contract $c$ matching query $q$ receives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:search}
\mathrm{score}(q,c)=\mathrm{BM25}(q,c)\cdot\bigl(\ln(e+|C|\,r_c)\bigr)^{\alpha},\qquad \alpha=1,
\end{equation}
where BM25 \cite{robertson2009bm25} runs over the contract name, the publisher scope, function and trait names from the ABI, comments in the source and the package README, and $|C|\,r_c$ equals one for a contract of average score. Results can be filtered by trait conformance (SIP-009, SIP-010, SIP-013), Clarity version and network.
