## Main research question (one sentence)

What do ERC-20 allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score for smart contracts on Arbitrum One, compared with the transfer walk and with the raw degrees that both walks smooth?

## Claims C1–C6 (Chapter 4 states them; Chapters 1, 5, 6 refer to them)

- **C1 (cost).** On the same contracts the solver's time follows the edge count of its graph. The allowance graph has about one-third of the transfer graph's edges (22.3 M vs 59.7 M), and EndorseRank's call takes correspondingly less time than AWP's; C-PR and S-PR walk the transfer layer and take longer than AWP. The speed belongs to the sparser graph, not to the algorithm. (Use benchmark numbers from the digest; report the time ratio next to the edge ratio.)
- **C2 (same-window agreement).** Each score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on: EndorseRank on the allowance family, AWP on the transfer family, with intervals far from zero; this is an intended-construct check and partly built in. Unlike a cohort dominated by ties, every contract in this cohort received approvals, so EndorseRank leaves almost no ties (0.15% of pairs) and the isolated-node rule changes no coefficient. The C-PR mixtures lie between their two layers on every family.
- **C3 (divergence).** EndorseRank and AWP order the contracts differently: τ = 0.217 [0.209, 0.225]; about 39% of the pairs ordered by both are discordant.
- **C4 (degree baselines out of window).** The two walks behave differently against the counts they smooth. AWP ranks later new transfer senders better than the raw transfer in-degree in both windows (W0 +0.038, W1 +0.020, intervals above zero), so on the transfer side propagation added information. EndorseRank, as fixed in the plan (uniform restarts), ranks new approval pairs below the raw in-approve degree in both windows (W0 −0.011, W1 −0.029). The variant with AWP's activity restarts ranks them above the degree in both windows (W0 +0.043, W1 +0.018), but that comparison was made after the labels were known and is a hypothesis for a registered test, not a finding. None of the W0 degree contrasts of EndorseRank and AWP was fixed in advance; The increment of C-PR(λ=1) over the in-approve degree (−0.025) and the two S-PR contrasts were also computed after the labels; only the two contrasts of rule A were fixed in the plan.
- **C5 (coupling).** One walk over both edge types does not beat its layers walked alone. Rule A fails: C-PR is no worse than the allowance walk on new approval pairs (+0.008) but worse than the transfer walk on new senders (−0.030); its same-window part fails as well. Rule B, registered before W1 was extracted, is not met: F1 holds (+0.101 over the transfer walk on new approval pairs, so the W0 exploratory gain of +0.094 replicates), F2 fails (−0.025, beyond the margin of 0.01), F3 fails (−0.044 on the traders' liquidation-free close rate). Against AWP (registered sensitivity) F2 fails clearly. S-PR stays near the transfer walk and far below the allowance walk on new approval pairs: allowance edges help only when they carry the walk.
- **C6 (outcomes built from neither edge type, registered).** On the liquidation-free close rate of GMX V2 contract traders, the registered count-level lead of the approval count over the transfer count (R1) is not supported in the primary window W1 (P = +0.063, 97.5% interval includes zero), although P was positive in W0 (+0.252); the PageRank lead (R2) is not supported; R3 applies (the approval count ranks traders lower on the profitable and non-loss shares). Every trader that had received an approval avoided liquidation in both windows (37 of 37, 40 of 40), against about three in four of the others, but the samples are small (70 and 88 contract traders) and the transfer count also ranks this label.

## Points the chapters must carry

- Multi-hop propagation exists in this graph: EndorseRank equals the closed form c(1 + d·δ(v)) within 1% for every spender whose owners receive no approvals (depth one), but only for about 35% of the spenders with a deeper in-neighbourhood; overall 86% of the cohort spenders at T_obs and 74% of the W0 spenders. Chapter 5 keeps the closed-form subsection, now as a statement about depth-one spenders, and says that multi-hop propagation is present here but did not make EndorseRank beat the raw count.
- Sybil model on this graph (13.1 M nodes): a closed farm collects about 3 times its population share at d = 0.85 (2.0 and 6.6 at 0.75 and 0.95); a star of 10 addresses reaches the cohort's 90th percentile region (13 farm addresses reach it), about 1,250 reach the 99th percentile, and the top contract would need about 1.08 million. Cheap to look ordinary or above median, expensive to reach the top.
- Contract GMX traders are few in 2026 (activity of contract accounts on GMX V2 fell after 2025), so every trader result has wide intervals; say so where the trader results appear.
- Top-token restriction shrinks every coefficient (EndorseRank allowance 0.481 → 0.237) but keeps the pattern; sample-definition stages move coefficients by up to 0.15 and are not robustness evidence.
- Coverage: 3,753 W0 spenders had no transfer edge at the freeze (AWP scores them zero); restricting to the 29,228 that had received a transfer leaves the degree comparisons in place.
- The cohort is the set of contracts approved by at least three owners; results apply to such contracts on Arbitrum One in these months.
- Revocations and the drain heuristic rise with every score and most with the in-approve degree; they track volume and are not offered as evidence.

- Self-destructed contracts: since EIP-6780 (cite ballet2023eip6780; give no activation date for Arbitrum One, which no source in the bib documents) SELFDESTRUCT no longer removes code except in the creating transaction, so a contract that self-destructed before that would read as an EOA at block 513,216,506; this is a small, stated limitation.
- Amount shares and timing are in describe/describe.json (digest section "Data description"): 99.52% of ego transfers and 99.78% of latest allowances are at least 10 base units; 45.3% of latest allowances are unlimited; 5,798,712 of 13,029,306 owners hold an unlimited allowance and 1,532,301 hold both kinds.
