\chapter{Implementation and Empirical Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter runs the design of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} on Arbitrum One as a single case study: one chain and one observation window, one matched cohort of contracts for every same-window comparison, spender and trader cohorts for the two holdout windows W0 and W1, and subsamples of the matched cohort for runtime against size. Rule~A, fixed in the analysis plan of 9~October~2026 before any log was extracted, is judged on W0 and in the same window. Rule~B and the comparison on outcomes built from neither edge type were registered after the W0 results were known and before the logs of W1 were extracted, and are judged on W1, with W0 as the secondary window of the comparison (Sections~\ref{sub:fresh-results} and~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}). Every table is generated from the evaluation summaries of Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}, each Kendall $\tau$ comes with its 95\% confidence interval, and each PageRank is read against the raw degree it smooths. Each section states which of its comparisons were fixed in advance.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:dataset]{Case study setting: Arbitrum One}

Arbitrum One was chosen because it is an Ethereum rollup on which many DeFi protocols run (Section~\ref{sec:ethereum-l2}), because its logs are in the public BigQuery export that the pipeline reads, and because one of its margin-trading protocols, GMX~V2, records the position closes behind the trader labels. The observation window runs from 1~October~2023 to 30~June~2026 (33 months), and the time decay is anchored at its end, $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$. An event at the anchor has time weight $0.858$; the weight falls to $0.5$ at 180~days and is close to zero for events more than two years old (Figure~\ref{fig:logistic-decay-methods}). Events from the early months still create edges, but the last year carries most of the edge weight in AWP and EndorseRank.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:cohorts]{Cohorts}

Four sets of contracts are scored or evaluated. Every address in them holds contract code; externally owned accounts (EOAs) enter the graphs as owners, senders and recipients and are never scored.

\begin{itemize}
\item Matched cohort ($n=27{,}844$). Every contract that received a non-zero \texttt{Approval} from at least three distinct owners in the observation window. The set was fixed from the approval logs and the account types before any transfer was aggregated (Section~\ref{sec:data-sources}). Of the 29,022 candidate spenders, \texttt{eth\_getCode} at block 513,216,506 (9~October~2026) returned contract code for 27,844, no code for 1,121 and an EIP-7702 delegation designator \parencite{buterin2024eip7702} for 57; the last two groups are EOAs and were left out. Membership does not depend on either graph at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$. Of the cohort contracts, 962 are outside the allowance graph, because by the end of the window the latest \texttt{Approval} of every owner and token that had approved them carried the value zero and they held no positive allowance as owners. The transfer graph leaves out 3,297, because no non-zero transfer in the window names them as sender or recipient (a spender that moves tokens with \texttt{transferFrom} from an owner directly to a third address does not appear in the resulting \texttt{Transfer} event), and 138 contracts are outside both graphs. A contract absent from a graph scores zero under that graph's method. Every same-window alignment, difference test and benchmark is computed on this cohort.
\item Spender cohorts of the two holdout windows. In W0 the scores are frozen at $t_1$ (31~March~2026, 23:59:59~UTC) and the labels are counted from 1~April to 30~June~2026. The cohort is every contract spender that holds at least one positive latest allowance at $t_1$ in the extracted approval data. Of the 33,556 such spenders, 33,101 hold contract code, 431 have no code and 24 carry an EIP-7702 designator; the 455 EOAs are left out. In W1 the scores are frozen at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (30~June~2026, 23:59:59~UTC), the labels are counted from 1~July to 30~September~2026, and the same rule gives 34,587 contract spenders. The spender cohorts are not subsets of the matched cohort. The extracted approvals include those that cohort contracts grant as owners, so the contracts they approve enter as spenders, and a spender needs only one owner with a positive allowance.
\item Trader cohorts. The GMX~V2 accounts that hold contract code, are nodes of the freeze-date graph (either layer) and close at least three positions in the label window: 88 in W0, 40 of which had received an approval by the freeze, and 70 in W1, 37 of which had. Contract accounts closed 133,685 GMX~V2 positions in the observation window, 6,473 of them by liquidation, with 88,002 closes in 2025 against 9,809 in the first half of 2026, and 6,805 from July to September~2026, 182 of them by liquidation. Every trader result therefore rests on few contracts and has wide intervals.
\item Subsamples of the matched cohort. Deterministic SHA256 subsamples (seed \texttt{contract-benchmark-v1}) with $n=1{,}000$, $2{,}000$, $5{,}000$, $10{,}000$ and $20{,}000$ contracts, followed by the full cohort (Section~\ref{sub:expanded-pool}). Each stage keeps the extracted edges incident to its sampled contracts, so its edge count grows with $n$, from 194,084 allowance and 1,385,509 transfer edges at $n=1{,}000$ to the full graphs. The stages serve the runtime against size (Section~\ref{sub:runtime-scaling}) and the sensitivity to the sample definition (Section~\ref{sub:sample-size}).
\end{itemize}

At $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the allowance graph has 13,055,150 nodes and 22,321,470 edges, and the transfer graph 23,029,298 nodes and 59,737,617 edges; at $t_1$ the two graphs have 21,550,791 and 58,065,784 edges. On the same contracts the transfer graph thus has 2.68 times as many edges as the allowance graph, which has 37\% of the transfer graph's edge count. An owner--spender pair is an allowance edge only while its latest allowance is positive, whereas every pair of addresses that exchanged a non-zero transfer in the window is a transfer edge. Of the allowance graph's nodes, 13,029,306 are owners with at least one positive allowance, so that graph consists almost entirely of owners pointing at a much smaller set of spenders. Both graphs come from the same extraction, the ego network of the cohort, so the differences between the scores trace back to their edges and restarts.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:implementation]{Implementation}

Every algorithm runs on one Python implementation of PageRank ($d=0.85$, $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ in the $L_1$ norm, $I_{\max}=300$). EndorseRank and AWP differ in their edge lists and restart vectors, and C-PR mixes its two edge lists node by node (Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition}). Each evaluation run writes its scores, per-proxy statistics and bootstrap intervals to one summary file, and the benchmark writes its timings to a file of its own. The same-window and W0 results were computed after the observation window had been extracted and before W1 was registered; the W1 results were computed after the July--September logs had been extracted. At $d=0.85$ every solve converged well inside the iteration cap: at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ EndorseRank needed 84 iterations, AWP 93, C-PR at $\lambda=0.5$ 92 and C-PR ($\lambda=0$) 98, and the longest single solve, the seeded transfer walk of S-PR, 102 (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark-results}). Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists the runs, the repository revision, the configuration file, the summary files and the machine on which the benchmark was timed.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:benchmark-results]{Computational benchmark (Claim~C1)}

Claim~C1 concerns what each score costs to compute on the same input. Following the timing procedure of Section~\ref{sec:computational-benchmark}, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} reports for each algorithm, on the full graphs of the matched cohort at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the mean solve time and its standard deviation over five timed runs after one untimed warm-up, the peak memory of the warm-up solve, the number of iterations, and the nodes and edges of the solver graph. Each graph and setting was timed once. The same measurement reappears as the full-cohort row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} and as the $d=0.85$ row of Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}, and Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runs} in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} lists every timed run.

\IfFileExists{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}{\input{results/tables/benchmark-runtime.tex}}{}

EndorseRank solves the allowance graph (13,055,150 nodes, 22,321,470 edges) in 29.6~s on average and AWP solves the transfer graph (23,029,298 nodes, 59,737,617 edges) in 69.8~s. AWP thus takes 2.36 times as long as EndorseRank, at an edge ratio of 2.68. Assembling the sparse matrix and running each power iteration are both linear in $|E|$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:pr-sparsity}), and the two solves need similar numbers of iterations (84 and 93, as in the evaluation runs at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$), so the time ratio is set mainly by the edge ratio, which it stays slightly below. Peak memory is 1,285.0~MB for EndorseRank and 2,995.9~MB for AWP, and the standard deviations over the five timed runs are 0.61~s and 2.30~s. The speed is a consequence of $|E|$: on the same contracts the allowance graph has 37\% of the transfer graph's edges, and both scores use the same solver.

The two hybrid rows show what the speed result covers and where it stops. C-PR walks on the union of both edge sets (25,304,106 nodes, 71,375,442 edges) and needs 105.5~s, 1.51 times as long as AWP on 1.19 times its edges. S-PR runs on the transfer layer, the graph of AWP, and takes 100.9~s, 1.44 times as long as AWP. Both its time and its iteration count include the solve of the allowance layer that supplies its restart vector; in the evaluation run at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$ its 185 iterations are 83 for that solve and 102 for the seeded transfer walk. The lower cost is thus confined to the score that walks the allowance graph alone and comes from that graph's size: any algorithm that walks the transfer layer, on its own or combined with allowance information, pays for the edges of that layer.

Two caveats attach to this result. The time covers only the solver call on edge lists built in advance; a full refresh would add parsing, decoding and edge-list construction, which all algorithms share wholly or in part. And a faster method is not necessarily a better one, so the runtime has to be read against the alignment and holdout results that follow.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:same-window-alignment]{Same-window alignment (Claim~C2)}

Claim~C2 asks what each score agrees with in the window it is computed from. Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid} reports the family-mean Kendall $\tau$ of every score on the transfer, allowance and Sybil-stability families, each with a 95\% paired-bootstrap interval. The raw degrees have no row because in this window they are proxies: in-degree belongs to the transfer family and in-approve degree to the allowance family. The secondary part of rule~A (Section~\ref{sec:holdout-protocol}) is the only same-window comparison that was fixed in advance; the paired contrasts below were not named in the plan and were computed together with the same-window results.

\input{results/tables/alignment-hybrid.tex}

The scores divide by the edges they walk on. EndorseRank reaches $0.481$ $[0.476,0.487]$ on the allowance family and $0.122$ $[0.113,0.129]$ on the transfer family; AWP reaches $0.447$ $[0.440,0.453]$ on the transfer family and $0.173$ $[0.165,0.180]$ on the allowance family. Each score is ahead of the other on the family of its own edges, EndorseRank by $+0.309$ $[0.300,0.317]$ on the allowance family and AWP by $+0.325$ $[0.315,0.335]$ on the transfer family (Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff} in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp}). The two layers of C-PR walked alone repeat the split: C-PR ($\lambda=1$) reaches $0.512$ $[0.506,0.517]$ on the allowance family and C-PR ($\lambda=0$) $0.528$ $[0.522,0.534]$ on the transfer family. On the Sybil-stability family the scores that walk on transfers lead, AWP with $0.311$ $[0.303,0.317]$ against $0.025$ $[0.019,0.031]$ for EndorseRank. Part of this agreement is built in. The main proxies of each family come from the same events as one of the graphs, in-degree and in-value from the transfers and in-approve degree and value from the latest allowances, and a PageRank smooths the in-degree of its own graph. Agreement with the proxies of a score's own edge type is therefore an intended-construct check and cannot count as an external test. The holdouts of Section~\ref{sec:holdout-results} ask the same question out of window.

Appendix~\ref{sec:proxy-family-detail} breaks the family means of EndorseRank and AWP into their proxies. AWP's largest coefficient is with transfer in-degree ($0.541$ $[0.534,0.547]$); with in-value it is lower ($0.353$ $[0.345,0.361]$). That is what its weights lead one to expect: with the value parameter used here (Section~\ref{sub:awp-graph}), the value weight $V$ is close to one for every transfer of ten base units or more, whatever its amount, so AWP follows the number of incoming transfers more closely than their summed value. On the Sybil-stability family AWP follows transfer tenure ($0.452$ $[0.445,0.458]$) and active months ($0.499$ $[0.492,0.506]$) and is close to zero on the inbound-counterparty ratio ($-0.019$ $[-0.029,-0.010]$).

EndorseRank follows the in-approve degree ($0.626$ $[0.620,0.632]$) much more closely than the in-approve value ($0.336$ $[0.329,0.344]$), since its value weight caps each allowance as AWP's caps each transfer. Its transfer coefficients are low, $0.196$ $[0.187,0.204]$ with in-degree and $0.048$ $[0.039,0.055]$ with in-value. On the Sybil-stability family it is negative on the inbound-counterparty ratio ($-0.128$ $[-0.135,-0.121]$) and low on tenure ($0.083$ $[0.074,0.090]$) and active months ($0.121$ $[0.113,0.129]$), so how many owners approve a contract says little about how long it has received transfers. C-PR ($\lambda=1$) walks the same allowance graph with raw amounts and also restarts uniformly; it stays close to EndorseRank on every family and is higher on the in-approve value ($0.420$ $[0.413,0.428]$), which counts raw amounts as its own layer does.

Because every contract in the cohort received approvals from at least three owners, EndorseRank leaves almost no ties here. It gives 25,650 distinct scores to the 27,844 contracts, and only 0.15\% of contract pairs are tied, most of them among the 962 contracts outside the allowance graph (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}). Its coefficients therefore rest on an ordering of nearly every contract, and keeping the contracts outside a graph as isolated nodes changes none of them (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}).

Claim~C2 is supported as an intended-construct check, with agreement that is partly built in. Every score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, with intervals far from zero, and the mixtures of C-PR lie between their two layers on every family (Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results}).

\dissertationSubsubheading[sec:coupled-results]{The coupled operator (RQ5)}

Two hybrids were defined in Section~\ref{sub:coupled-graph}. The coupled operator C-PR mixes the raw-amount allowance and transfer layers in each node's transition row with weight $\lambda$, and the endorsement-seeded walk S-PR keeps the transfer layer but restarts from the allowance walk. Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid} gives their paired contrasts in this window: first eight against the two layers walked alone, then four against EndorseRank and AWP. RQ5 is decided out of window (Sections~\ref{sec:holdout-results} and~\ref{sub:fresh-results}). Here we only locate the hybrids on the checks that the single-layer scores were built to pass.

\input{results/tables/tau-diff-hybrid.tex}

C-PR lies between its two layers on every family. At $\lambda=0.5$ it reaches $0.296$ $[0.289,0.303]$ on the transfer family, $0.418$ $[0.412,0.423]$ on the allowance family and $0.129$ $[0.124,0.134]$ on the Sybil-stability family, and as $\lambda$ rises from $0.25$ to $0.75$ it moves toward the allowance layer on all three (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). It stays nearer to its allowance layer than to its transfer layer: on the allowance family it falls $0.094$ below C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[-0.099,-0.088]$), and on the transfer family $0.232$ below C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[-0.239,-0.224]$). The mixing rule of Equation~\eqref{eq:coupled-transition} acts at every node with out-edges in both layers. Almost every node of the allowance layer is an owner with out-edges, so the allowance rows enter the walk wherever an owner also sent a transfer that the extraction kept. Against the single-layer scores C-PR is below AWP on the transfer family ($-0.151$, $[-0.159,-0.142]$) and below EndorseRank on the allowance family ($-0.063$, $[-0.069,-0.058]$). On the Sybil-stability family it lies above the allowance walk ($+0.109$, $[0.105,0.113]$) and EndorseRank ($+0.103$, $[0.099,0.108]$) and below the transfer walk ($-0.182$, $[-0.189,-0.176]$) and AWP ($-0.182$, $[-0.189,-0.175]$).

On every family S-PR falls below the single layer that leads that family. Restarting the transfer walk from the allowance walk lowers the transfer coefficient to $0.467$ $[0.460,0.473]$ ($\Delta\tau=-0.061$, $[-0.065,-0.057]$, against C-PR ($\lambda=0$)) and the Sybil-stability coefficient to $0.298$ $[0.291,0.304]$ ($-0.013$, $[-0.015,-0.011]$). It raises the allowance coefficient to $0.320$ $[0.313,0.328]$, against $0.195$ $[0.187,0.202]$ for the transfer walk with uniform restarts (no paired contrast was computed for this pair), yet leaves it $0.191$ below the allowance walk ($[-0.198,-0.184]$). A restart vector changes where the walk lands after each teleport. It carries part of the allowance signal into the transfer walk, but less than walking the allowance edges does.

As fixed in the analysis plan, the secondary part of rule~A asks C-PR's point estimate at $\lambda=0.5$ to lie within the 95\% interval of the better layer on the transfer and allowance families, and C-PR to exceed both layers on the Sybil-stability family. All three parts fail. The transfer coefficient ($0.296$) lies below the interval of C-PR ($\lambda=0$) ($[0.522,0.534]$), the allowance coefficient ($0.418$) lies below that of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) ($[0.506,0.517]$), and on the Sybil-stability family C-PR exceeds only the allowance layer. The paired contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}, a stricter check that is not part of the rule, point the same way. In the same window the mixture gives up part of each layer's agreement with its own family and exceeds the better layer on no family. The holdout takes up the remaining question of whether it has predictive content that the single layers lack.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:rank-divergence]{Rank divergence (H1 / RQ1)}

Hypothesis~H1 and RQ1 concern whether EndorseRank and AWP put the same contracts in different orders. The inter-method Kendall $\tau_b$ between the two score vectors is $0.217$, with interval $[0.209,0.225]$ (last row of Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}), so the interval excludes both zero and one. The two rankings are related, yet far from interchangeable. Of the 387,630,246 contract pairs, 98.5\% are ordered by both scores, and 39.0\% of these are ordered in opposite directions.

Ties play almost no part in this result. The marginal score distributions are in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}. EndorseRank gives 25,650 distinct scores to the 27,844 contracts and ties 0.15\% of the pairs; AWP gives 24,545 distinct scores and ties 1.40\%. Under each score the largest tie block is formed by the contracts outside its graph, which score zero: 962 under EndorseRank and 3,297 under AWP, 138 of them under both. Under EndorseRank 370 further contracts share one score, $c=3.40\times10^{-8}$, the score of a node without in-edges; they are in the allowance graph only as owners. Every other cohort contract has received at least one positive allowance and scores above $c$. A marginal distribution cannot show how two scores order the same contracts, so Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter} plots the two rankings against each other for a random sample of 3,000 contracts, with tied contracts at their mid-rank as in $\tau_b$. The zero blocks appear as a column at the right edge (EndorseRank) and a row at the top (AWP). Contracts that both scores rank near the top, or both near the bottom, lie close to the identity line; in between, the points spread over most of the square.

The zero blocks come from one rule of the pipeline: a contract missing from a graph scores zero (Section~\ref{sec:graph-construction}). The analysis plan fixed the alternative, keeping missing contracts as isolated nodes, as a sensitivity analysis, and Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity} gives the result. No coefficient changes at the third decimal, and the agreement between the two scores stays at $0.217$ $[0.209,0.225]$. The reason is where the missing contracts land. Under EndorseRank's uniform restarts an isolated node receives $c$, the lowest score that any node of the graph can have, so the 962 contracts stay at the bottom of the order and only join the 370 contracts at $c$. Every pair that the zero rule ordered keeps its order, and only the pairs between the two groups become ties. Under AWP's restarts an isolated contract that sent nothing receives no restart mass and scores zero under both rules. Because the cohort was defined by approvals received, nearly every cohort contract is a spender in the allowance graph, and the rule for missing contracts decides almost nothing.

H1 is supported. The two scores order the cohort differently, and the difference does not come from ties or from the rule for missing contracts. A protocol that relies on transfer-based scores cannot assume that its ordering of contracts survives a switch to allowance-based scores or a blend with them.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:robustness-results]{Robustness and sensitivity checks}

Three checks, run for EndorseRank and AWP, vary the damping factor, the token mix and the sample definition. The analysis plan fixed the damping values and both token selections; the sample-definition check was not in the plan. The first two checks hold the sample fixed and ask whether the results hinge on one setting. The third changes the sample itself, so its coefficients are expected to move, and it is not evidence of robustness. A fourth check asks how much EndorseRank depends on its restart rule.

\dissertationSubsubheading[sub:damping]{Damping}

Table~\ref{tab:robustness-damping} recomputes the family means at $d\in\{0.75,0.85,0.95\}$ on the same graphs.

For EndorseRank no family mean moves by more than $0.003$ across the three values; its allowance coefficient is $0.483$ $[0.478,0.488]$ at $d=0.75$ and $0.480$ $[0.474,0.485]$ at $d=0.95$. AWP moves most on the Sybil-stability family, from $0.315$ $[0.308,0.321]$ to $0.303$ $[0.296,0.310]$, and its transfer coefficient falls from $0.448$ $[0.441,0.455]$ to $0.444$ $[0.437,0.451]$. Neither score's pattern is an artifact of the conventional $d=0.85$. EndorseRank's stability is largely structural. For a spender whose owners receive no approvals, EndorseRank equals $c\,(1+d\,\delta(v))$, with $\delta(v)$ the owner-normalized in-strength (Section~\ref{sub:theory-closed-form}), and the order that this formula gives among such spenders does not depend on $d$; 20,942 of the 26,512 cohort contracts with in-edges are spenders of this kind. The number of iterations grows with $d$, from 50 to 84 and 224 for EndorseRank and from 54 to 93 and 276 for AWP. The runtimes are 19.0, 29.6 and 63.6~s for EndorseRank and 38.6, 69.8 and 162.6~s for AWP, time ratios of 2.03, 2.36 and 2.56 at an edge ratio of 2.68, on the full graphs of Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} (13,055,150 nodes and 22,321,470 edges for EndorseRank, 23,029,298 and 59,737,617 for AWP); each graph and damping pair was timed once, and the $d=0.85$ values are the main measurement, reused. A spender with many owners sees more of everything owners do, revocations and drains included, so these labels cannot separate trust from use.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:empirical-claims]{Claims (C1--C6)}

The chapter's results support the claims below, cross-referenced to the hypothesis and research question columns of Table~\ref{tab:hrq-claim-map}. C1--C5 rest on the main analyses, and C6 on the registered comparison of Section~\ref{sub:neutral-label-results}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[C1.] Cost. On the same contracts the solver's time follows the edge count of its graph. The allowance graph has 37\% of the transfer graph's edges (22,321,470 against 59,737,617 at $T_{\mathrm{obs}}$), and EndorseRank's call takes correspondingly less time than AWP's: AWP takes 2.36 times as long (69.8~s against 29.6~s), at an edge ratio of 2.68 (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}). C-PR and S-PR walk the transfer layer and take longer than AWP. The speed belongs to the sparser graph, not to the algorithm.
\item[C2.] Same-window agreement. Each score agrees most with the proxies of the edge type it walks on, EndorseRank with the allowance family ($0.481$, $[0.476,0.487]$) and AWP with the transfer family ($0.447$, $[0.440,0.453]$), with intervals far from zero (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-hybrid}). This is an intended-construct check, and the agreement is partly built in. Every contract in the cohort received approvals, so EndorseRank leaves almost no ties (0.15\% of pairs), and the isolated-node rule changes no coefficient (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}). The C-PR mixtures lie between their two layers on every family.
\item[C3.] Divergence. EndorseRank and AWP order the contracts differently: $\tau_b=0.217$ $[0.209,0.225]$, and about 39\% of the pairs ordered by both are discordant (Table~\ref{tab:tie-sensitivity}, Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}).
\item[C4.] Degree baselines out of window. The two walks behave differently against the counts they smooth. AWP ranks later new transfer senders better than the raw transfer in-degree in both windows (W0 $+0.038$, $[0.035,0.042]$; W1 $+0.020$, $[0.016,0.023]$), so on the transfer side propagation added information. EndorseRank, as fixed in the plan (uniform restarts), ranks new approval pairs below the raw in-approve degree in both windows (W0 $-0.011$, $[-0.016,-0.006]$; W1 $-0.029$, $[-0.032,-0.025]$). The variant with AWP's activity restarts ranks them above the degree in both windows (W0 $+0.043$, $[0.036,0.049]$; W1 $+0.018$, $[0.012,0.023]$), but that comparison was made after the labels were known and is a hypothesis for a registered test, not a finding (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:fresh-posthoc}). None of the W0 degree contrasts of EndorseRank and AWP was fixed in advance, and the W1 ones were not named in the registration and were computed after the July--September labels were known. The increment of C-PR ($\lambda=1$) over the in-approve degree ($-0.025$, $[-0.030,-0.019]$) and the two S-PR contrasts were also computed after the labels; only the two contrasts of rule~A were fixed in the plan. Restricting W0 to the spenders that had received a transfer leaves both degree comparisons in place (Table~\ref{tab:holdout-receiving}).
\item[C5.] Coupling. One walk over both edge types does not beat its layers walked alone. Rule~A fails: C-PR is no worse than the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($+0.008$, $[0.003,0.013]$) but worse than the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.030$, $[-0.034,-0.026]$), and its same-window part fails as well (Tables~\ref{tab:holdout-diff} and~\ref{tab:tau-diff-hybrid}). Rule~B, registered before W1 was extracted, is not met (Table~\ref{tab:fresh-contrasts}). F1 holds ($+0.101$, $[0.092,0.111]$, over the transfer walk on new approval pairs), so the W0 exploratory gain of $+0.094$ ($[0.085,0.103]$) replicates; F2 fails ($-0.025$, $[-0.029,-0.021]$, beyond the margin of 0.01); and F3 fails ($-0.044$, $[-0.100,-0.002]$, on the traders' liquidation-free close rate). Against AWP, in the registered sensitivity analysis, F2 fails clearly ($-0.087$, $[-0.093,-0.082]$). In two W0 contrasts computed after the labels were known, S-PR stays near the transfer walk on new senders ($-0.009$, $[-0.012,-0.006]$) and below the allowance walk on new approval pairs ($-0.051$, $[-0.060,-0.043]$): allowance edges help only when they carry the walk. Section~\ref{sec:coupled-results} reports where the hybrids stand within the window.
\item[C6.] Outcomes built from neither edge type (registered). On the liquidation-free close rate of GMX~V2 contract traders, the registered count-level lead of the approval count over the transfer count (R1) is not supported in the primary window W1 (P $=+0.063$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.040,0.158]$), although P was positive in W0 ($+0.252$, 97.5\% interval $[0.067,0.438]$). The PageRank lead (R2) is not supported (Q $=+0.051$, 97.5\% interval $[-0.104,0.232]$). R3 applies: the approval count ranks traders lower than the transfer count on the profitable share ($-0.226$, $[-0.421,-0.038]$) and the non-loss share ($-0.272$, $[-0.444,-0.108]$). Every trader that had received an approval avoided liquidation in both windows (37 of 37 and 40 of 40), against about three in four of the others, but the samples are small (70 and 88 contract traders), and the transfer count also ranks this label ($0.331$, $[0.208,0.441]$, in W1) (Tables~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts} and~\ref{tab:neutral-label-recipients}).
\end{enumerate}

The rank divergence answers RQ1, the same-window tables RQ2, the benchmark tables RQ3, and the holdouts RQ4 and RQ5; C6 adds no research question. The direct comparisons between EndorseRank and AWP are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-er-awp} and Table~\ref{tab:neutral-label-contrasts}, and none of them carries a claim of the thesis. Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} reads these findings against the main research question, what ERC-20 allowance edges add to PageRank as a reputation score for smart contracts on Arbitrum One compared with the transfer walk and with the raw degrees that both walks smooth, and against the objectives of Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}.
