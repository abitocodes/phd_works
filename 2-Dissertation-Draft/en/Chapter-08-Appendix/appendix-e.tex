\section{Claim-to-Evidence Map}
\label{ch:appendix-claims}

This appendix maps each hypothesis, research question, and empirical claim to the specific evidence in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. It is intended as a navigation aid for examiners and replicators.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h1]{H1 / RQ1 --- Rank divergence}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Statement:} EndorseRank and AWP yield distinct wallet orderings on the same cohort.

\item \textbf{Evidence:} Inter-method Kendall $\tau=0.316$ between score vectors (Section~\ref{sec:rank-divergence}); rank scatter in Figure~\ref{fig:rank-scatter}; score distributions in Figure~\ref{fig:score-distributions}.

\item \textbf{Interpretation:} Related but not interchangeable rankings.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h2]{H2 / RQ2 --- Construct-specific alignment}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Statement:} Social methods differ in which proxy families they align with.

\item \textbf{Evidence:} Table~\ref{tab:alignment-three-methods}; per-family Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer}--\ref{tab:alignment-gmx}; heatmap Figure~\ref{fig:alignment-heatmap}.

\item \textbf{Winners:} EndorseRank on allowance ($\tau=0.355$ vs.\ $-0.030$); AWP on transfer ($0.534$ vs.\ $0.333$) and trading success among social methods ($0.179$ vs.\ $0.096$).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-h3]{H3 / RQ3 --- Computational efficiency}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Statement:} EndorseRank is faster than AWP at equal $n$.

\item \textbf{Evidence:} Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime} ($2.105$\,s vs.\ $18.711$\,s, $\approx 8.9\times$); Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-scaling} ($\approx 10.4\times$ at $n=10{,}000$; $\approx 9.4\times$ at $N=31{,}612$); Figure~\ref{fig:runtime-scaling}; edge counts $14{,}727$ vs.\ $137{,}087$.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-rq4]{RQ4 --- Validation frame}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Statement:} For social methods, transfer proxies align more strongly than trading-success proxies on this chain.

\item \textbf{Evidence:} Table~\ref{tab:alignment-three-methods}: transfer means $0.333$ (ER) and $0.534$ (AWP) exceed trading-success means $0.096$ (ER) and $0.179$ (AWP).
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-srq1]{SRQ1 / SC1 --- GF-PR ceiling}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Statement:} GF-PR bounds trading-outcome alignment via construct overlap.

\item \textbf{Evidence:} Table~\ref{tab:alignment-three-methods} row GF-PR: inverse-risk $0.376$, trading success $0.583$; Section~\ref{sec:gf-pr-ceiling}.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-c1c4]{Claims C1--C4}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1:} runtime evidence as in H3/RQ3.
\item \textbf{C2:} credible alignment on selected proxies---allowance for ER, transfer for AWP---in Tables~\ref{tab:alignment-allowance} and~\ref{tab:alignment-transfer}.
\item \textbf{C3:} construct-specific winners as in H2/RQ2.
\item \textbf{C4:} efficiency--alignment trade-off combining C1 with allowance $\tau=0.355$ and transfer $\tau=0.333$ for EndorseRank; robustness in Tables~\ref{tab:robustness-damping}--\ref{tab:robustness-sample-size} and Figure~\ref{fig:damping-sensitivity}.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-nonclaims]{Explicit non-claims}

The dissertation does \emph{not} claim:

\begin{itemize}
\item that EndorseRank or AWP predicts trading profits from social graphs alone;
\item that AWP is obsolete;
\item that GF-PR is a deployable social reputation score;
\item that results generalize beyond Arbitrum One, GMX V2, and the study window without replication;
\item that on-chain scores replace collateral or legal identity systems.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:map-artifacts]{Artifact checklist for replication}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Merged wallet-ranking table --- scores for ER, AWP, GF-PR.
\item Decoded GMX close events with PnL and liquidation flags.
\item Machine-readable evaluation summary --- authoritative numerics.
\item Exported LaTeX table fragments.
\item Exported vector figures.
\item Optional archived extended-baseline matrices.
\end{enumerate}

Replicators can regenerate tables and figures by running the evaluation pipeline on real data with robustness sweeps and table export, then regenerating figures from the same artifacts.
