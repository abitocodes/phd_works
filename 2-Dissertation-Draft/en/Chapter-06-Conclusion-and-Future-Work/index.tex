\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This chapter synthesizes this research, states the original contribution to knowledge, revisits the research questions, and outlines future work on cross-protocol validation, adversarial manipulation, hybrid methods, and live oracle integration.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:summary-findings]{Summary of findings}

This research formulated EndorseRank, a PageRank method on a latest-allowance endorsement graph that produces an on-chain reputation score on Arbitrum One, and evaluated it against Adaptive Weighted PageRank (AWP), the transfer-graph baseline of Do et al.\cend{16}, on an identical matched wallet cohort ($n=5{,}521$) with non-zero endorsement- and transfer-graph connectivity. Contemporaneous checks used transfer and allowance statistics. A temporal holdout scored wallets through 28~February~2026 and labeled t1 inbound spenders ($n=1{,}335$) by new owner--spender approvals in March--May~2026.

The primary findings for the EndorseRank--AWP comparison are as follows.

On reputation structure (RQ1), EndorseRank and AWP yield partially overlapping yet construct-distinct orderings. Inter-method Kendall $\tau$ between the two score vectors is $0.316$: correlation is present, but allowance and transfer graphs emphasize different wallet relationships. H1 is supported.

On social-method alignment (RQ2), construct-specific winners emerge. EndorseRank leads on allowance checks ($\tau=0.355$ versus AWP $-0.030$); AWP leads on transfer checks ($\tau=0.534$ versus EndorseRank $0.333$). H2 is supported in its construct-specific form: neither social method universally dominates.

On computational efficiency (RQ3), EndorseRank requires less PageRank wall-clock time than AWP on the matched cohort and on the expanded wallet pool. At $n=5{,}521$, runtimes are $2.105$\,s versus $18.711$\,s ($\approx 8.9\times$). Pool scaling preserves the advantage at $n=10{,}000$ ($\approx 10.4\times$) and at $N=31{,}612$ ($\approx 9.4\times$). Robustness checks on damping and top-token subgraphs do not overturn the efficiency--alignment pattern. H3 is supported (Claim~C1); together with alignment results, this feeds Claim~C4.

On the temporal holdout (RQ4), EndorseRank aligns with future new approvers at Kendall $\tau=0.479$ (CI $0.426$--$0.529$) on the spender cohort, while AWP is near zero ($\tau=0.044$). That is out-of-window evidence for the endorsement construct.

Collectively, Claims~C1--C4 establish an efficiency--alignment trade-off: EndorseRank offers substantially lower PageRank cost, stronger allowance-construct validity, and holdout evidence on future new approvals, while AWP offers stronger transfer alignment. The appropriate choice depends on whether operators prioritize authorization semantics and refresh latency or transfer-hub detection.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:contribution-knowledge]{Original contribution to knowledge}

This research contributes to on-chain reputation research and DeFi risk assessment in four respects.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{EndorseRank:} An allowance-based endorsement graph model. By treating latest ERC-20 \texttt{approve} and EIP-2612 \texttt{permit} authorizations as directed endorsement edges \cend{18,19}, it extends PageRank's citation metaphor \cend{49,8} from transfer graphs \cend{16} to explicit spending delegation. The model eliminates temporal decay through allowance override semantics and yields a sparse graph ($14{,}727$ edges on the matched cohort) suitable for frequent recomputation.
\item \textbf{Construct checks and temporal holdout:} Compares EndorseRank and AWP on contemporaneous transfer and allowance statistics \cend{14,54,36} and on t2 new owner--spender approvals after scores are frozen at t1.
\item \textbf{Reproducible open-source evaluation pipeline:} Links extraction, graph construction, scoring, checks, and LaTeX-ready tables to a machine-readable summary (Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval}). Auditability of decentralized credit-related scores is a recognized concern \cend{48}; the pipeline makes the empirical claims independently verifiable.
\end{itemize}

Relative to prior graph-reputation and DeFi credit work \cend{35,27,29,16,39,44}, the original knowledge contribution is the demonstration that allowance-graph PageRank is a distinct, efficient, and allowance-valid social reputation construct on Arbitrum One---not a drop-in approximation of transfer-graph AWP, and not a substitute for collateralization \cend{26,53,28,59}.

Authorization graphs and payment graphs are therefore not interchangeable reputation substrates. They differ in sparsity ($14{,}727$ versus $137{,}087$ edges on the matched cohort), runtime ($2.105$\,s versus $18.711$\,s), and alignment profiles (allowance $\tau=0.355$ for EndorseRank; transfer $\tau=0.534$ for AWP), and they should be validated against proxies that match their intended semantics \cend{14}.

\dissertationSubheading[sec:future-work]{Future work}

Several extensions follow from the limitations in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} and the scope of this study.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cross-protocol and cross-chain validation:} Replication on other venues and Layer-2 or Layer-1 networks would test whether the allowance--transfer trade-off and the holdout pattern generalize beyond Arbitrum One \cend{53,59}. Alternative L2s offer natural replication sites with comparable event schemas.

\item \textbf{Adversarial evaluation:} The Sybil cost model in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} is analytical. Empirical red-teaming---including wash approvals, coordinated spender farms, and strategic revocation---would quantify realized attack cost-effectiveness under live fee markets and detection heuristics \cend{17}. Sybil clustering on $G_{\text{approve}}$ and comparison with transfer-graph manipulation costs are natural next experiments. Such work would also stress-test whether Sybil-adjusted stability proxies remain informative under active adversaries.

\item \textbf{Hybrid social methods:} Operators may combine endorsement-graph features with transfer-graph features, or with DeFi-informed representation learning \cend{39}, while preserving the auditability emphasized by Packin and Lev-Aretz\cend{48}. Hybrid designs should report construct-specific checks and time-split evidence rather than a single aggregate metric.

\item \textbf{Live oracles and governance tooling:} Integration with risk oracles and governance dashboards would test whether EndorseRank's runtime profile ($2.105$\,s on the matched cohort; order-of-magnitude gains on the expanded pool) sustains production refresh cadences. Careful interface design is required so that ordinal graph scores are not over-interpreted as consumer credit ratings. Human-in-the-loop safeguards remain appropriate wherever scores gate access to financial services.

\item \textbf{Collateral-differentiation experiments:} Section~\ref{sub:deploy-industry} argues that protocols can capture value through fee tiers, utilization, and monitoring cost without treating reputation as a collateral substitute. Until controlled evidence exists, collateral reduction remains an adoption hypothesis rather than an empirical claim of this research.

\item \textbf{Adoption pathway and reputation multiples:} Longitudinal studies of protocols that publish recomputable social ranks would clarify whether a shared ordinal vocabulary spreads across venues in the manner of valuation multiples (Section~\ref{sub:deploy-multiples}). Metrics of interest include cross-protocol score correlation, fee-tier uptake, monitoring-cost deltas, and Sybil-filter efficacy under live reward programs.

\item \textbf{Archived baselines and richer authorization semantics:} Extended baselines archived in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix-eval} can be revisited when additional social-graph semantics are needed. Richer authorization events---including protocol-specific delegation logs where available---may refine endorsement edge definitions beyond ERC-20 allowances alone. Longer observation windows and additional market regimes would further stress-test stability of the reported $\tau$ profiles.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading[sec:closing-reflection]{Closing reflection}

On-chain reputation will not dissolve the need for collateral in adversarial, pseudonymous markets \cend{53,26}. It can, however, make behavioral structure legible. This research shows that legibility depends on which edges we choose to read. Transfers tell a story about economic flow; allowances tell a story about delegated authority. PageRank can narrate either story, but not both at once with the same ranking. EndorseRank commits to the authorization narrative and documents its costs and benefits, including a time-split check of later new approvals.

The practical upshot is modest and actionable. If a protocol or researcher needs a fast, auditable score of who is trusted to spend, EndorseRank is a credible instrument on Arbitrum One data of the kind studied here. If the goal is to find transfer hubs, AWP remains stronger among social methods. If the goal is to predict trading profits from social graphs alone, that question is outside this research's claims.

This research reports primary empirical claims C1--C4 addressing RQ1--RQ4 on a matched Arbitrum One wallet cohort ($n=5{,}521$) and a spender holdout ($n=1{,}335$). EndorseRank stands as an efficient, allowance-specific alternative to transfer-based AWP: distinct in ranking geometry (inter-method $\tau=0.316$), aligned with its primary construct (allowance $\tau=0.355$), approximately $8.9\times$ faster on the studied sample, and aligned with future new approvers at $\tau=0.479$. Those properties motivate continued research on authorization-aware trust signals in decentralized markets.
