\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\dissertationSubheading{Findings by research question}

\textbf{RQ1 (reputation structure):} EndorseRank and AWP produce divergent orderings (inter-method Kendall $\tau = 0.316$). Allowances encode authorization intent while transfers encode economic flow; the sparser $G_{\text{approve}}$ graph yields distinct centrality from $G_{\text{transfer}}$.

\textbf{RQ2 (trading-success alignment):} On GMX success proxies, AWP achieves higher mean $\tau$ than EndorseRank ($0.179$ vs.\ $0.096$; Table~\ref{tab:alignment-gmx}). Allowance-based propagation does not align more strongly with profitable perpetual closes on this cohort than transfer-based AWP.

\textbf{RQ3 (efficiency):} EndorseRank's runtime advantage (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-runtime}; $\approx 2.9$\,s vs.\ $\approx 24$\,s) follows from latest-allowance snapshots instead of aggregating full transfer history with decay. This supports near-real-time reputation refresh for DeFi risk dashboards.

\textbf{RQ4 (validation frame):} Transfer proxies yield higher mean $\tau$ than GMX success for both methods (Table~\ref{tab:alignment-summary}). Margin-trading outcomes and liquidation metrics show weak-to-modest alignment overall, suggesting that no single proxy family fully captures ``reputation'' on this sample.

\textbf{RQ5 (cross-family comparison):} EndorseRank outperforms AWP on \emph{allowance} proxies ($\tau=0.355$ vs.\ $-0.030$) but not on transfer or most outcome families. Results support construct-specific validity rather than universal dominance of either method.

\dissertationSubheading{Theoretical implications}

The six-proxy framework extends Do et al.'s validation logic by separating transfer centrality, allowance delegation, leveraged outcomes, liquidation stress, and Sybil-adjusted activity. EndorseRank aligns with its native allowance construct; AWP aligns with transfer and several outcome proxies derived from the same wallet activity.

\dissertationSubheading{Practical implications}

Protocols requiring transfer-hub detection may prefer AWP despite higher cost. Protocols emphasizing explicit spending authorization or low-latency refresh may prefer EndorseRank, accepting lower transfer-proxy alignment. The open pipeline enables cross-protocol replication on any ERC-20 chain with allowance logs.

\dissertationSubheading{Threats to validity}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Proxy not ground truth:} No under-collateralized lending default labels; GMX liquidation and PnL are domain-intuition proxies, not loan repayment.
\item \textbf{Sybil and wash trading:} Adversarial allowance or transfer patterns may manipulate rankings; Sybil-adjusted proxies partially address but do not eliminate this risk.
\item \textbf{Single protocol and chain:} Generalization to other perpetual or lending protocols requires cross-protocol validation.
\end{itemize}

\dissertationSubheading{Limitations}

Results reflect one six-month window on Arbitrum One. Token-decimal normalization, USD price oracles, and permit-vs-approve coverage may affect edge weights. Professor-approved scope and ethics constraints from \texttt{1-Proposal/proposal/} apply unchanged.
